Solar system

The Sun

  • Star Type: G2 V yellow dwarf
  • Surface Temperature: ~5,500 °C
  • Corona Temperature: Up to 2 million °C
  • Composition: 71% H, 27% He, 2% other
  • Distance from Earth: 149 million km
  • Rotation Period: ~25–35 Earth days

Formation

The Sun formed about 4.6 billion years ago from a collapsing cloud of gas and dust called the solar nebula. Most of the material gathered at the center to form the Sun, while the rest formed planets and other objects.

In about 5 billion years, the Sun will become a red giant, potentially engulfing Mercury, Venus, and Earth, before eventually becoming a white dwarf.

Interesting facts about the Sun

  • About 100 times wider than Earth and responsible for 99.8% of the solar system's total mass.

  • Actually white, but Earth's atmosphere makes it appear yellow or orange by scattering blue light.

  • Temperatures range from about 15 million °C in the core to 5,500 °C at the surface.

  • Made of extremely hot plasma, with no solid surface to stand on.

  • Sunlight takes about 8 minutes and 20 seconds to reach Earth.

  • Rotates faster at the equator, taking about 25 days, compared with about 35 days near the poles.

  • Travels around the Milky Way at about 720,000 km/h, completing one orbit every 230 million years.

  • Formed about 4.6 billion years ago and is expected to shine for roughly another 5 billion years.

Moons

Our solar system has more than 891 moons. Many moons orbit planets, and even some asteroids have moons. Here are the most famous of them.

The Moon

The Moon is Earth's only natural satellite and orbits our planet at an average distance of about 384,400 km. It reflects sunlight and goes through different phases as it moves around Earth. The Moon also affects ocean tides and was first visited by humans in 1969 during the Apollo 11 mission.

Io

Io is a moon of Jupiter and the most volcanically active body in the Solar System. Its surface is covered with hundreds of volcanoes that constantly erupt sulfur and lava. These eruptions give Io its bright yellow, orange, and red colors.

Europa

Europa is a moon of Jupiter covered with a thick layer of ice. Scientists believe there is a vast liquid ocean beneath its icy surface. Because of this hidden ocean, Europa is considered one of the most promising places to search for possible life beyond Earth.

Ganymede

Ganymede is the largest moon in the Solar System and orbits Jupiter. It is even bigger than the planet Mercury and is the only moon known to have its own magnetic field. Scientists believe that Ganymede may also have a subsurface ocean beneath its icy crust.

Titan

Titan is the largest moon of Saturn and the second-largest moon in the Solar System. It has a thick atmosphere and lakes made of liquid methane and ethane on its surface. Titan is one of the most Earth-like worlds beyond our planet.

Enceladus

Enceladus is a small moon of Saturn covered mostly with ice. It is famous for powerful geysers that shoot water vapor and ice particles into space. Scientists believe there may be a liquid ocean beneath its icy surface.

Triton

Triton is the largest moon of Neptune. It has a very cold surface covered with ice and is known for its cryovolcanoes, which erupt nitrogen gas instead of lava. Triton is unique because it orbits Neptune in the opposite direction of most moons.

Phobos and Deimos

Phobos and Deimos are the two small moons of Mars. They are irregularly shaped and are believed to be captured asteroids. Phobos orbits very close to Mars, while Deimos is smaller and moves farther away from the planet.
